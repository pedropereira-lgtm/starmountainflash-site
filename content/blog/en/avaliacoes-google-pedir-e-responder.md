Some review tools first ask the customer "were you happy?" and only send them on to Google if they tap yes. Anyone who taps no ends up on an internal form. It looks like a clever way to protect your rating, but Google prohibits it.

## The rules

The Google Maps [contribution policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) doesn't allow incentives in exchange for reviews, whether money, a discount or a free product. Nor does it allow discouraging negative reviews or asking only some customers for positive ones.

In other words, you ask everyone and give nothing in return.

## Why it's worth the effort

[BrightLocal's 2026 survey](https://www.pinmeto.com/news/brightlocal-local-consumer-review-survey-2026/) asked 1,002 consumers in the United States. I couldn't find an equivalent study for Portugal, but one figure helps explain the behaviour: 74% look for reviews from the last three months.

That changes how you think about it. Asking for reviews for a week and then forgetting leaves the profile looking stale before long. What counts is asking every customer, every time, without relying on anyone's memory.

## How to ask

The best moment is right after the job. The Google Business Profile has its own link for requesting reviews, and that's the one to put in the message, so the customer doesn't have to search for the business.

A message like this is enough:

> Hello Mr Rui, thank you for trusting us with the windows in your home. If you have a minute, your opinion helps other people decide: [link]. Pedro

It's short, it has the customer's name and it says what was done. Better still if it's sent by the person who did the work.

## How to reply

Positive reviews deserve a reply too, a real one. In the same survey, 80% say they prefer businesses that reply to every review, and half are put off by generic replies. A "Thanks for choosing us!" pasted under forty reviews shows.

With negative reviews, the person who reads your reply is the next customer:

> Ms Ana, I'm sorry about the delay with the installation. You were right: we should have told you the materials were late before the scheduled day. I'd like to talk to you and sort out what's still pending. Could you call me on [number]?

It acknowledges what went wrong, doesn't argue in public and takes the conversation off the page. A fake or offensive review can't be deleted, but it can be reported to Google if it breaks the policies.

For companies with shops or branches in several cities, each address has its own profile and its own reviews. Someone searching in Coimbra sees the Coimbra shop's rating, not head office's. What usually goes wrong is some shops replying to everything and others never replying at all.

In [Google reviews](https://starmountainflash.pt/en/avaliacoes-google) I set up this system: the request goes out on its own after every job and every review gets a reply. The profile review is free.
