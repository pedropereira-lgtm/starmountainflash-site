"Automation" has become a word people use for everything, which makes it almost useless in practice. Not every business needs to automate anything, and trying to automate too early only creates complexity where there was no problem in the first place. Having built systems for local businesses, I keep seeing the same pattern: the signs that automation is worth it are fairly concrete.

## You do the same task by hand every week

Answering the same kind of request, copying information from one place to another, letting someone know every time something happens: if you can describe the process step by step because you have already done it dozens of times, that is exactly the kind of task that no longer needs you in the middle of it.

## Your information is scattered across several places

One request comes in on WhatsApp, another by email, another through the [website form](/en/blog/ligar-formulario-site-crm-email), and at the end you have to pull it all together by hand so nothing slips through. When the information isn't all in one place, the risk of forgetting someone goes up, and that usually costs more than the time it would take to build the automation.

## Growth is held back by the time you spend on admin

If the reason you don't take on more clients is "I don't have time to manage any more of this", it is worth working out how much of that time is work that genuinely needs your attention, and how much is just repetition.

## When it isn't worth it yet

If the process is still changing every week, or only happens now and then, automating too early tends to be time badly spent: the process hasn't settled down enough to be worth locking into a system. In that case, the priority is to understand the process properly by hand first, and only then build around it.

If you recognise at least one of the first three signs, you are probably already losing time on something that didn't need your direct attention. See [the kind of automations I build for SMEs](/en/automacoes).
