While going through Search Console for starmountainflash.pt, I opened a report called Generative AI. It shows how many times the site appeared in Google's AI answers: in my case, 41 times in three months.

Next I went to Google Trends to see whether more people in Portugal are searching this way. "Modo ai" and "modo ia" (AI Mode) are among the AI-related searches that grew the most over the past year. AI Mode reached Portugal in October 2025. It's a tab on the results page: you type a long question and get a written answer, with links to a few sites.

**Short answer:** AI Mode relies on the same Google search systems to choose the pages it cites. A company shows up there when its site is indexed, answers customers' questions clearly and is confirmed by other sources. Search Console's Generative AI report, available since June 2026, shows how often that has happened.

## How people search in AI Mode

In a regular search, someone types "aluminium window frames Paredes". In AI Mode they write something like "I need to replace the windows in a warehouse in Paredes, which frames handle the cold best and who does this locally". According to Google, questions in AI Mode are two to three times longer than traditional searches.

To answer, Google splits the question into several topics and runs many searches at once, on frame types, insulation and local companies, then pulls it all together. A page can be cited for answering one of those parts well, even if it would never rank first for a short search. For a small company that's unlikely to beat someone with twenty years in the trade for "window frames Paredes", that opens a door.

When it isn't confident enough in the written answer, Google shows regular results instead. Google itself admits AI Mode still gets things wrong.

## AI Mode, AI Overviews and ChatGPT

AI Overviews are the summary that appears at the top of regular results for some searches. AI Mode is a separate tab, where the person can keep asking follow-up questions. ChatGPT is a different tool, with different crawlers and rules, and I've written about it in [what ChatGPT needs before it recommends your company](https://starmountainflash.pt/en/blog/aparecer-no-chatgpt-empresa-portugal).

For Google, AI Overviews and AI Mode follow the same rules: no special files and no new markup. A page that can appear in regular search can be used in AI answers.

## What the report showed for my site

Between August and October 2026, starmountainflash.pt appeared 41 times in Google's AI answers. The home page accounted for 35 of those and the StudyOS case study for 6. Blog posts got one or two each, but almost all of them are only a few weeks old.

These are small numbers from a new site, and you can't draw rules from them. But they help decide what to work on first. The home page is being used as a summary of who I am, so its first paragraph has to say what I do and for whom, plainly. And the StudyOS case study, where I explain in detail how the platform was built, was cited more than service pages with more general copy. That's why I'm expanding the text of the other case studies.

Almost all the appearances came from Portugal, and 37 of the 41 were on desktop. My guess is that these are searches made at work, but the report doesn't let me confirm it.

## How to check whether your company already appears

In Search Console, open **Performance** and, just below it, **Generative AI**. The report shows how many times the site appeared in AI Overviews and AI Mode, by page, country, device and day. The questions people asked are left out. If the report isn't there, the most likely reason is that the site doesn't have enough appearances yet.

Then test by hand. Open a private window, go to the AI Mode tab and type questions the way one of your customers would, with the area, the type of job and the deadline. Note who shows up and repeat a month later. Answers change depending on who is searching and from where, so compare months rather than single results.

## What helps a page get cited

First of all, the site has to be in Google's index. If it isn't, it won't show up anywhere, and the first step is understanding [why your site doesn't show up on Google](https://starmountainflash.pt/en/blog/site-nao-aparece-no-google).

Once that's sorted, what the page says is what counts. It helps to have one page per service that answers the main question right at the start, with information AI can repeat without making things up: which areas you cover, how long a typical job takes, what the process looks like. Case studies with detail on what was done, like the StudyOS one on my site, carry weight too.

Outside the site, the information has to match. If your Google Business Profile has one set of opening hours, your site another, and a directory still shows the old address, AI merges it all and gets the answer wrong. [Google reviews](https://starmountainflash.pt/en/blog/avaliacoes-google-pedir-e-responder) are part of the same picture.

## What you can't control

There is no way to guarantee your company appears in an answer. Google changes its systems without warning, and the same question gives different answers to different people.

It's also possible to appear and not get the click, because the written answer may be enough. So when someone contacts you, it's worth asking how they found you. It's the only way to know whether these appearances are bringing anything in.
