On Google Trends, "regulamento sobre inteligência artificial" (AI regulation) is among the AI-related searches that grew the most in Portugal over the past year. Many business owners use ChatGPT or Claude every day and don't know whether that puts them under any obligation. I had to work out what applies to me too, because I use these tools in my own work and build AI automations for other companies. Here is what already applies and what has been pushed back.

**Short answer:** for most small businesses, what already counts is telling customers when they are talking to an AI, labelling AI-generated images and videos that look real, and taking steps so the team knows how to use these tools. The strictest rules, for high-risk systems such as screening job candidates, were postponed to December 2027 and August 2028.

I'm writing this as someone who implements automations. For a specific case, talk to a lawyer.

## What the regulation is

[Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj), known as the AI Act, sorts AI systems by risk level. The higher the risk to people, the more rules apply. Most of what a small business uses day to day (writing emails, summarising documents, answering frequent questions) sits at the lower levels.

The regulation came into force in 2024 and applies in phases. In July 2026 it was amended by a second regulation, known as the Digital Omnibus, which came into force on 27 July and changed some deadlines and obligations.

## What already applies

### The team has to know how to use AI

Article 4 requires companies that use AI to take steps so their team knows how to work with it. The July amendment made the wording more flexible. The company has to do something, suited to people's experience and how they use the tools, but it doesn't have to guarantee a minimum level of knowledge.

In a small business this can be simple: one page listing the tools the team uses, what can and can't be put into them, and who reviews things before they go to a client.

### Transparency

Since 2 August 2026, the transparency rules in Article 50 apply. For a small business, the chatbot is what matters most: people talking to it have to know it's an AI. AI-generated or AI-altered images, audio and video that look real also have to be labelled as such. There is also a technical obligation for tools that generate content to mark it in a way other systems can detect.

That last part falls on the companies that build the tools, such as OpenAI, Anthropic or Google. Generative systems already on the market before August have until 2 December 2026 to comply.

Fines for breaking these rules can reach 15 million euros or 3% of worldwide turnover. For small and medium-sized businesses, the regulation applies whichever of the two is lower.

## What was postponed

High-risk systems are those that can seriously affect people's lives. For a small business, the most likely examples are using AI to screen CVs or make hiring decisions, or to assess whether a customer is creditworthy.

The rules for these systems were due to apply in August 2026. They were postponed to 2 December 2027. AI systems built into products that are already regulated, such as machinery or medical devices, moved to 2 August 2028.

If your company already uses AI to filter candidates, you have a year to prepare. A good start is documenting how the system decides and making sure a person reviews every decision.

## The GDPR still applies

The AI Act doesn't replace the GDPR. If someone on the team pastes a customer's email, an invoice with a name and tax number, or health data into a chatbot, that's processing personal data. Before using these tools with customer data, check what kind of account you have and how the provider handles data. Business accounts usually come with different rules from personal ones.

## Checklist

- You have a list of the AI tools your team uses and what for.
- There is a written rule, even a short one, about what data doesn't go into these tools and who reviews what comes out.
- The chatbot on your website or WhatsApp says at the start that it is an automated assistant and shows how to reach a person.
- AI-generated images and videos used in advertising that look real are labelled.
- If you use AI to screen candidates or assess customers, you already know which rules you'll have to meet in December 2027.
