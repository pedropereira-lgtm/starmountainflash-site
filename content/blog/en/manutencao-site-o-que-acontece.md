When a website is finished, it's natural to think the job is done. It has been paid for, it's live and customers can see it. The problem is that a website depends on several parts that change over time: the hosting, the domain, the security certificate, the service that sends the form emails, the plugins and Google itself. When one of them changes and nobody is keeping track, the site still looks the same from the outside, but it stops doing what it was built to do.

The dangerous part is that almost none of these failures give any warning. These are the ones I see most often, and what you can check yourself.

## 1. The contact form stops sending requests

This is the most expensive failure and the quietest one. The form is still on the site, the customer fills it in, clicks "Send" and even sees a success message. The request just never reaches your inbox.

It can happen for several reasons: you changed your email address or email provider, the password of the account that sends the messages was changed, or the messages started going to spam. For weeks, the only clue is the feeling that "the website has stopped bringing in enquiries".

**How to check:** fill in your own form using a different email address and confirm whether the request arrives and where. Do this once a month.

## 2. The security certificate expires

The SSL certificate is what puts the padlock and the "https" in the address bar. When it expires, the browser warns visitors that the site is not secure, and most people close the page right there.

Many hosting providers renew the certificate automatically, but that renewal can also fail, for example after a server move or a change to the domain settings.

**How to check:** open the site in a browser and click the padlock next to the address. The certificate's expiry date is shown there.

## 3. The domain lapses

The domain (your "companyname.pt") is rented for a set period, usually one year, and has to be renewed. If the card on file has expired or the renewal reminders go to an inbox nobody reads, the domain lapses and the website and the company's email stop working at the same time.

The domain should always be in the company's name, in an account you have access to, not in the name of whoever built the site.

**How to check:** find out which account the domain is registered in, whose name it is in, and the next renewal date. Put it in your calendar a month ahead.

## 4. Updates are left undone

Websites built on WordPress, or on other platforms with plugins, receive regular updates. Many of them fix security flaws that are already publicly known, which means that people looking for vulnerable sites know about them too.

An out-of-date site can be used to send spam, redirect visitors to other pages or simply go down. The opposite carries risks as well: updating everything at once without a backup can break the site.

**How to check:** log into the site's dashboard and see whether there are pending updates. If there are many and they have been waiting a long time, don't click "update all" before taking a backup.

## 5. There is no backup, or there is one but it has never been tested

A backup only matters on the day you need it: after an attack, an update that went wrong or someone deleting a page by mistake. On that day, the question is whether there is a recent copy and whether it can be restored.

Many hosting providers say they take backups, but they keep only a few days' worth, or store them on the same server as the site. If the server fails, the backup goes with it.

**How to check:** ask whoever manages your hosting how often backups are taken, where they are stored and when anyone last restored one.

## 6. The information goes out of date

This one isn't technical, but it loses customers just the same. Old opening hours, a phone number that no longer exists, services you no longer offer or a news section whose last post is two years old. To a visitor, all of this says the business has stalled.

**How to check:** read the site as if you were a customer who has never heard of you. Check contact details, opening hours, address and the list of services.

## A 15-minute monthly routine

If nobody is looking after your website, this list covers the essentials:

- Send a test request through the form and confirm it arrives.
- Check the certificate's expiry date in the browser padlock.
- Confirm the domain's renewal date.
- Check for pending updates and when the last backup was taken.
- Review contact details, opening hours and services.
