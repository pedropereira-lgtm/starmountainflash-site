**Short answer:** to test an idea or have a simple online presence on a minimal budget, Wix is enough. For a website with lots of pages, a blog or a shop, which the business wants to control in the long run, WordPress is the most common choice. A bespoke website makes sense when the site has to do something the platforms do not handle well: integrations, client areas, demanding performance or a design that does not fit a theme.

## What is each option?

- **Wix:** a website builder. You put the site together by dragging blocks around in an online editor, and you pay a monthly subscription that includes hosting.
- **WordPress:** the most widely used content management system in the world, found on around 43% of all websites ([W3Techs, via Barn2](https://barn2.com/wordpress-market-share/)). The software is free, but it needs hosting, a theme and, almost always, plugins.
- **Bespoke website:** written in code by a developer, with no theme or builder. Every page and every feature is built for that project.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Wix | WordPress | Bespoke |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | The lowest | Medium | The highest |
| Monthly costs | Plan subscription | Hosting, paid plugins and maintenance | Hosting and maintenance |
| Who updates it | The owner, in the editor | The owner, in the dashboard | Content management panel, if included in the project |
| Design freedom | Limited to the editor's blocks | Limited by the theme, or open with custom development | Complete |
| Integrations (CRM, invoicing, bookings) | Apps from the Wix store | Plugins | Direct connection, built to measure |
| Technical maintenance | Handled by Wix | Frequent plugin and theme updates | Fewer parts to update |
| Changing platform | You cannot take the site with you | You can change hosting provider | You can change hosting provider |

## When is Wix enough?

When the business needs to get online quickly, with only a few pages, and wants to make changes on its own. For a business that is just starting out, a simple presentation page on Wix is better than having no website at all.

The limit comes on the day you want to leave. [Wix itself explains](https://support.wix.com/pt/article/exportar-ou-incorporar-o-site-wix-em-outro-lugar) that a site built on the platform cannot be hosted anywhere else. The content is yours and the domain can be transferred, but the site itself has to be rebuilt.

## When is WordPress the best choice?

When the website is going to grow in pages and content: an active blog, lots of service pages, or a shop with WooCommerce. There are themes and plugins for almost everything, and plenty of professionals who know how to work with it, which makes it easier to switch provider.

The hidden cost is maintenance. In 2025, 11,334 new vulnerabilities were found in the WordPress ecosystem, 91% of them in plugins ([Patchstack](https://www.patchstack.com/whitepaper/state-of-wordpress-security-in-2026/)). A WordPress site without regular updates is a real risk. The more plugins, the more parts to maintain.

## When is a bespoke website worth it?

When the site has to do something specific that the platforms do not handle well:

- **Integrations:** forms that save to a database and notify the CRM, connections to invoicing or booking software.
- **Client or member areas**, with account-based access.
- **Performance and technical control:** without the weight of themes and plugins that load code the site does not use.
- **An original design**, one that does not end up looking like thousands of other websites.

The downside is the upfront investment and depending on someone who can code. That is why the project should include a panel for editing text and articles without touching the code.

## How to decide in three questions

1. **What does the website need to do, beyond presenting the business?** If the answer is "nothing", Wix or a simple WordPress site will do.
2. **Who is going to handle updates and security?** If nobody, avoid a WordPress site loaded with plugins.
3. **Where do you want to be in three years' time?** If the plan is to grow, choose a solution you can take with you.
