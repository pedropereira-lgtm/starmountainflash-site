On Google Trends, "claude" and "claude ai" are among the AI-related searches that grew the most in Portugal over the past year. I use Claude every day at Starmountain, for client work and to run the business itself. This article brings together what I see working in a small company and the precautions worth taking before your team starts using it.

**Short answer:** Claude is an AI assistant made by Anthropic, similar to ChatGPT, that writes, summarises and analyses documents in Portuguese. In a small business it saves time on emails, proposals, meeting summaries and reading long documents. Before using it with customer data, pick the right plan and set out what can and can't go into it.

## What Claude is

It's an AI assistant, like ChatGPT or Gemini, built by Anthropic. You use it in the browser or the app: you type a request, you can upload documents, and it answers. For someone starting out the difference between them is small. They all do the same kind of work, and the result depends more on how you ask and who reviews it than on the brand.

## Tasks where it saves a small business time

**Emails and quote requests.** You paste the customer's request and your rough notes, and it prepares an organised reply. You adjust the tone and the figures.

**Meeting summaries.** From a meeting transcript, it separates what the client asked for, the deadlines and each person's tasks. An hour-long meeting becomes a summary you can read in two minutes.

**Long documents.** A construction tender specification, the terms of a public tender or a supplier contract. You ask it for the deadlines, the penalties and what bidders have to provide, and you check against the original. It helps you read faster, but it doesn't replace a lawyer.

**Proposals and sales copy.** You give it the main points and an example of one of your proposals, and it builds a first version in the same style.

**Reviewing.** Website copy, a product description, a catalogue. Ask it to point out confusing sentences and mistakes rather than rewrite everything.

## How I use it in my business

Client meetings are transcribed. Claude then analyses the transcript and returns what the client wants, what I need to do and a draft of the follow-up email.

Prospecting runs overnight: it looks for companies that match the client profile I defined and leaves email drafts in Gmail. In the morning I read them, fix what needs fixing and decide which ones to send.

For visual proposals, I use it to build the first version of a website prototype based on the company's current site. Then I work on top of that.

In every case the rule is the same: Claude prepares and I decide what reaches the client.

## Data precautions

Since 2025, on Claude's personal plans (Free, Pro and Max), each user chooses whether their conversations can be used to train the models. If they accept, Anthropic keeps the data for up to five years; if not, the period is 30 days. Business plans (Team and Enterprise) and the API are not included in that training use.

For a company this means two things. If the team will work with customer data, a business plan makes more sense than everyone using a personal account. And whatever the plan, the GDPR still applies: customers' names, tax numbers, addresses and health data shouldn't go in without a reason and without rules.

There are AI-specific obligations too. I explained what a small business has to comply with in [the EU AI Act for small businesses](https://starmountainflash.pt/en/blog/regulamento-ia-pme).

## When chat is no longer enough

Chat solves a lot, but it has a limit: someone has to copy information in and copy the result somewhere else. When that happens every day, with the same steps, the task is ready to be [automated](https://starmountainflash.pt/en/automacoes). The meeting summary can go straight into the CRM, and a request coming in through the website can produce a draft reply without anyone copying anything.

When the problem is finding information in the company's documents, the answer is different: an [assistant that answers from your internal documents](https://starmountainflash.pt/en/blog/assistente-ia-documentos-empresa), with controlled access.

If you don't know where to start, the [Flash Diagnostic](https://starmountainflash.pt/en/starmap) is built for that: a week of logging the team's tasks, and at the end a list of what is worth automating first.
